# 👅 Specialty Coffee Tasting & Flavour Guide

Developing a palate for specialty coffee is a journey of sensory training. Coffee contains over 800 aromatic compounds (double that of wine!), making it one of the most chemically complex foods we consume.

---

## 1. The Anatomy of Taste & Flavor
Tasting is the combination of **taste** (tongue) and **aroma** (nose). 

### 👅 The 5 Basic Tastes (On the Tongue)
1. **Sweetness:** The holy grail of specialty coffee. High-quality arabica beans harvested at peak ripeness are naturally sweet. It feels like cane sugar, honey, or caramel.
2. **Acidity:** Not the same as sourness. Pleasant acidity gives coffee its brightness and life. Think of the crispness of a green apple, the juiciness of a peach, or the zip of a lemon.
3. **Bitterness:** Essential for structure, but it should be pleasant (like dark chocolate or black tea) rather than harsh (like ash or medicine).
4. **Sourness:** Often a sign of under-extraction. It tastes sharp, vinegary, or salty, causing the sides of your tongue to salivate excessively.
5. **Umami/Salty:** Rarely present in good brews. Saltiness usually points to severe under-extraction or water mineral imbalances.

### 👃 Aromatics (Olfaction)
- **Orthonasal Olfaction:** Sniffing the dry coffee grounds or the steam rising from the cup.
- **Retronasal Olfaction:** The aromas that travel from the back of your throat up to your nasal cavity *while* you swallow or hold the coffee in your mouth. This is how you identify complex notes like "jasmine," "blueberry," or "bergamot."

### 🥛 Tactile (Mouthfeel / Body)
Mouthfeel is the physical weight and texture of the liquid on your tongue:
- **Light/Tea-like:** Common in light-roast washed Ethiopians (especially on a V60).
- **Silky/Juicy:** A smooth, balanced weight (often found in Origami or clean naturals).
- **Creamy/Heavy:** Thick, coating mouthfeel (classic espresso or Moka pot).

## 2. Step-by-Step Tasting Technique

To build consistency, follow this ritual when drinking a cup of specialty coffee:

1. **Smell the Dry Grounds (Fragrance):** Grind the beans and shake the container. Sniff immediately. What does it remind you of? (Nuts, spices, berries?)
2. **Smell the Wet Grounds (Aroma):** Pour water and smell the steam. The heat releases volatiles that aren't soluble at room temp.
3. **The Slurp (Cupping Technique):** Let the coffee cool to around **60°C - 65°C** (warm, not scalding). Take a spoon, draw some coffee, and **slurp it vigorously**.
   - *Why?* Slurping vaporizes the liquid into a fine mist, coating your entire palate and sending molecules straight to your retronasal receptors.
4. **Follow the Temperature Drop:** Do not drink it all at once! Specialty coffee changes dramatically as it cools:
   - **Hot (65°C+):** Sweetness and body are prominent.
   - **Warm (50°C-60°C):** Acidity and complex fruit/floral flavors peak.
   - **Cool (below 50°C):** The cup becomes cleaner, sweetness intensifies, and defect or roast flavours (if any) become obvious.

## 3. The Extraction Compass (Tuning Your Brew)

Understanding how compounds extract helps you adjust your grind size and temperature:

```
☕️ START OF BREW  ==============================>  END OF BREW ☕️
Acid & Sour Acids  --->  Sweet Sugars & Oils  --->  Bitter & Dry Tannins
(Fastest to dissolve)      (Middle phase)           (Slowest to dissolve)
```

| Brew State | Characteristics | What to do |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Under-extracted** | Sour, salty, metallic, thin, quick finish. | **Extract more:** Grind finer, increase water temp, or increase brew time. |
| **Balanced (Sweet Spot)** | Sweet, ripe acidity, complex, clean, lingering finish. | **Perfect!** Save your recipe. |
| **Over-extracted** | Bitter, dry/astringent (like dry wine/tea), empty, hollow. | **Extract less:** Grind coarser, decrease water temp, or reduce brew time. |

## 4. Sensory Training Exercises

Palate development is muscle memory for your brain. Try these exercises at home:

1. **Side-by-Side Tasting (Triangulation):**
   - Brew two different coffees (e.g., a natural process Ethiopian and a washed Colombian) side-by-side using the same recipe. 
   - Even if you can't name the notes, the contrast will make the differences in acidity, body, and sweetness immediately obvious.
2. **The Fruit Bowl Calibration:**
   - When you buy fruit (e.g., blueberries, lemons, peaches, apples), smell them deeply. Eat them slowly and pay attention to *where* on your tongue you feel the acidity and sweetness. 
   - Compare this feeling to your coffee. If a coffee has "malic acidity," it feels like the crisp, clean snap of an apple.
3. **The Water Test:**
   - Try tasting different bottled waters or tap water to notice how minerals alter mouthfeel and bitterness. Soft water highlights acidity; hard water dampens it.
