# Pour Over Coffee Techniques (V60 and Origami)

Filter brewing is all about clarity of flavor. Both the Hario V60 and the Origami Dripper are cone-shaped, bypass-style brewers. They demand good pouring technique but reward you with clean, highly detailed tasting notes.

---

## 1. V60 vs. Origami: The Differences

While they look similar, they behave differently due to their physical design:

| Feature | Hario V60 | Origami Dripper |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Material** | Ceramic, Glass, Plastic, or Metal. | Porcelain or AS Resin. |
| **Interior Ribs** | Spiral ribs that channel water downward and allow air escape. | 20 deep, vertical grooves that maximize airflow and speed up drawdown. |
| **Paper Filters** | Cone-shaped V60 papers only. | **Dual compatibility:** Can use V60 cone papers OR Kalita Wave (flat-bottom) papers. |
| **Flavor Profile** | High acidity, high clarity, structured body. | **With V60 paper:** Extremely clean, bright, fast flow. <br>**With Wave paper:** Sweeter, higher body, more uniform extraction. |

---

## 2. Key Brew Variables

To make your brews repeatable, control these variables:

1. **Water Temperature:** 
   - **Light Roasts (Specialty Standard):** Use boiling water (93°C - 96°C). Light roasts are dense and hard to extract; high heat is necessary.
   - **Medium/Dark Roasts:** Lower temp (88°C - 92°C) to avoid extracting bitter, roasty compounds.
2. **Grind Size:** Medium-fine (texture of table salt or sandy cornmeal).
3. **Ratio:** The standard starting ratio is 1:15 to 1:16.6 (e.g., 15g of coffee to 250g of water).

---

## 3. Method A: Tetsu Kasuya's 4:6 Method
*Best for: Coarser grinds, customizable sweetness/acidity, and high sweetness.*

This method uses a coarser grind (like coarse sea salt) and divides the total water into five distinct pours. The theory is:
- **First 40% of water** adjusts the acidity and sweetness.
- **Remaining 60% of water** adjusts the strength (TDS).

### The Recipe (20g coffee to 300g water - 1:15 ratio)
*Grind size: Coarse. Temp: 92°C - 94°C.*

| Step | Time | Water Added | Total Weight | What it controls / Notes |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Pour 1 (Bloom)** | 0:00 | 50g | 50g | **Acidity control.** (A smaller 1st pour = more sweetness. Equal 1st & 2nd = balanced). |
| **Pour 2** | 0:45 | 70g | 120g | **Sweetness control.** (Larger 2nd pour highlights sweetness). |
| **Pour 3** | 1:30 | 60g | 180g | Strength phase. Let water drain completely between pours. |
| **Pour 4** | 2:15 | 60g | 240g | Strength phase. |
| **Pour 5** | 3:00 | 60g | 300g | Final strength phase. Let it drain completely (target 3:30 - 4:00 total drawdown). |

## 4. Method B: Hoffmann Ultimate 1-Cup Method
*Best for: Light roasts, maximum flavor extraction, and high clarity.*

This method focuses on maintaining water temperature in the brewer and achieving uniform extraction through gentle agitation.

### The Recipe (15g coffee to 250g water - 1:16.6 ratio)
*Grind size: Medium-fine. Temp: 95°C+ (boiling).*

1. **Bloom (0:00 - 0:45):**
   - Pour 45g of water quickly.
   - Pick up the dripper and swirl it gently until all grounds are wet. Let it bloom for 45 seconds to release CO2.
2. **Phase 1 Pour (0:45 - 1:15):**
   - Pour up to 150g in circular motions to create turbulence. Finish this pour by 1:15.
3. **Phase 2 Pour (1:15 - 1:40):**
   - Pour slowly in the center up to 250g. Do not pour on the paper filter edges (creates bypass).
4. **Agitate & Swirl (1:40 - end):**
   - Stir gently once clockwise and once counter-clockwise to scrape coffee off the walls.
   - As the water drains, give the brewer a gentle swirl (the "Rao Spin") to settle the coffee bed flat.
   - Let it drain (drawdown should finish around 2:30 - 3:00). A flat bed indicates uniform extraction.

---

## 5. Troubleshooting Filter Brews

- **Water takes too long to drain (Drawdown > 4 mins):** Your grind is too fine, or your paper is clogging with "fines" (micro-dust from grinding). Grind coarser, or pour more gently.
- **Drawdown is too fast (< 2 mins) and taste is sour:** Grind is too coarse. Grind finer.
- **Coffee tastes astringent (dry finish):** Channeling occurred (water bypassed the coffee bed). Focus on pouring slowly in circular motions, keeping the bed flat, and swirling at the end.
