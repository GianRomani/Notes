# Espresso, Milk Texturing and Latte Art (Sage Barista Express and Moka)

Espresso is a high-pressure extraction method that yields a highly concentrated beverage. Steaming milk and pouring latte art adds a visual and tactile dimension to your coffee.

---

## 1. Dialing In Espresso (Sage Barista Express)

The Sage Barista Express (SBE) is a capable machine, but its built-in grinder has relatively large steps. Here is how to dial in a new bag of beans.

### Standard Starting Parameters
* **Dose (In):** 18g of coffee in the double-shot single-wall basket.
* **Yield (Out):** 36g - 40g of liquid espresso in the cup (approx. 1:2 ratio).
* **Time:** 25 to 30 seconds (including pre-infusion).

### Step-by-Step Dialing Workflow
1. **Prep the Basket:** Grind 18g. Use a WDT tool (needle distributor) to break up clumps. Tamp level and firm.
2. **Manual Mode Extraction:** SBE allows manual control:
   - Press and hold the **Double Shot** button. This starts low-pressure pre-infusion.
   - Release the button at **8 seconds** to ramp up to full pump pressure.
   - Press the button again to stop the shot when your scale reads **36g**.
3. **Analyze the Shot:**
   - **Too Fast (< 20s):** Tastes sour, salty, and watery. **Action:** Grind finer (lower number on the dial).
   - **Too Slow (> 35s or dripping):** Tastes bitter, dry, ashy, and burnt. **Action:** Grind coarser (higher number).
   - **Channelling:** If you see spurts or a broken stream, improve your puck prep (WDT and even tamping).

## 2. Steaming Milk on the Barista Express

The SBE uses a thermocoil, which means steam pressure is lower than commercial dual-boilers. Patience and timing are key:

1. **Purge the Steam Wand:** Turn the steam dial on. Let it sputter out water until dry steam comes out. Turn it off.
2. **Positioning:** Insert the wand tip about 0.5 cm into the milk, slightly off-center (at the 3 o'clock position) to create a vortex.
3. **Phase 1: Stretching (Injecting Air):** Turn the dial on. You should hear a gentle paper-tearing sound.
   - Do this for about 5-8 seconds for flat whites, or 10-15 seconds for cappuccinos. The milk volume should expand by about 20-30%.
4. **Phase 2: Rolling (Integrating Air):** Raise the pitcher slightly so the wand tip is fully submerged.
   - The milk should spin in a fast whirlpool (vortex). This breaks big bubbles into microscopic bubbles (microfoam).
   - Keep spinning until the bottom of the pitcher is too hot to hold for more than 2 seconds (~60°C - 65°C). Turn off steam before removing the pitcher.
5. **Groom the Milk:** Tap the pitcher on the counter and swirl it. The milk should look like glossy wet paint.

## 3. Latte Art Mechanics

Latte art is all about height, speed, and angle of the pour.

```
    Phase 1: Canvas Creation              Phase 2: Drawing
      (High, slow pour)                 (Low, fast, tilted pour)
       
          \     /                            \     /
           \   /                              \===/   <-- Pitcher close
            | |                                | |
            | |  <-- Pitcher high             (   )  <-- Crema surface
         (       )
```

### The Three Basic Patterns

#### 1. The Heart
1. **Create Canvas:** Pour from 5-10cm above the cup in a circular motion to mix milk and crema. Fill the cup 50% full.
2. **Drop & Tilt:** Bring the pitcher nozzle as close as possible to the liquid surface, aiming at the center. Increase flow rate. A white cloud will appear.
3. **The Cut:** Once the cup is 90% full, raise the pitcher high, slow down the flow, and strike a line straight through the center.

#### 2. The Tulip
1. Create canvas to 50%.
2. Bring nozzle close, pour a small heart/cloud and stop pouring.
3. Move slightly back, pour a second cloud that pushes the first one forward. Stop pouring.
4. Pour a third cloud inside the second one.
5. Lift and cut through all layers to create a tulip.

#### 3. The Rosetta
1. Create canvas.
2. Bring nozzle close, start pouring, and gently shake your wrist side-to-side to create ripples.
3. As the ripples form, slowly back the pitcher out toward the edge of the cup.
4. Lift, slow the flow, and cut through the center.

## 4. The Moka Pot: How to Avoid Bitter Brews

The biggest mistake with the Moka pot is burning the coffee grounds with excessive heat. Follow these steps:

1. **Pre-heat Water:** Fill the bottom chamber with already boiling water up to just below the safety valve.
2. **Grind:** Medium-coarse (similar to sand, coarser than espresso).
3. **Fill Basket:** Fill it to the brim but do not tamp. Just level it off with a finger.
4. **Assemble Safely:** Screw the top chamber on using a towel (the bottom will be hot!).
5. **Heat Control:** Put it on the stove on low to medium-low heat. Keep the lid open.
6. **Stop the Cook:** When coffee begins to flow out, look for when it starts to bubble/foam and turn golden-yellow.
7. **Cool Down:** Immediately run the base of the pot under cold tap water to stop extraction and prevent bitter, metallic flavors. Pour immediately.
