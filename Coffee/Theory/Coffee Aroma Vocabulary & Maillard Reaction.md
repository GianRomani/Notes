# Coffee Smell Vocabulary & The Maillard Reaction
#coffee/sensory #coffee/roasting #coffee/theory #note

Smell is the primary driver of coffee flavor perception—over **80% of what we perceive as "taste"** is derived from direct (orthonasal) and throat-to-nasal (retronasal) olfaction.

![[coffee_aroma_wheel.jpg]]

---

## 1. The 4 Olfactory Stages in Coffee

* 🌿 **Fragrance:** The scent of freshly ground, dry coffee beans before hot water is added.
* ♨️ **Aroma:** The volatile vapors released when hot water saturates the coffee (e.g., breaking the crust in cupping).
* 👅 **Nose / Retronasal:** Aromatic compounds perceived through the back of the palate and throat while sipping and swallowing.
* ⏳ **Aftertaste / Finish:** Lingering flavor vapors remaining in the mouth, throat, and breath after swallowing.

### Sensory Defects & Off-Smells
* 🌾 **Grassy / Baked / Bready:** Under-developed roast or stalled roast heat.
* 🪵 **Burlap / Baggy / Musty:** Past-crop beans, high humidity storage, or mold.
* 🥜 **Peanut Shell / Quaker:** Unripe cherries picked prematurely.
* 🧪 **Phenolic / Medicinal / Rubbery:** Fermentation defects or Rio-zone fungal contamination.

## 3. The Maillard Reaction in Coffee Roasting

![[maillard_reaction_coffee.jpg]]

The **Maillard Reaction** is a non-enzymatic chemical reaction between **amino acids (proteins)** and **reducing sugars (glucose, fructose)** when exposed to heat (typically between **150°C and 200°C / 300°F–392°F**).

$$\text{Reducing Sugars} + \text{Amino Acids} \xrightarrow{\text{Heat (150°C - 200°C)}} \underbrace{\text{Aroma Volatiles (Pyrazines, Furans)}}_{\text{Complexity \& Aroma}} + \underbrace{\text{Melanoidins}}_{\text{Brown Color \& Crema Body}}$$

### Key Contributions to the Cup

1. **Creates Signature Roast Aromas:**
   * **Pyrazines & Pyrroles:** Nutty, roasted, toasted bread, and biscuit notes.
   * **Furans & Furanones:** Sweet, caramel, and baked goods aromas.
   * **Strecker Degradation:** Volatile aldehydes producing delicate savory and fruity nuances.
2. **Generates Melanoidins (Body & Crema):** High-molecular-weight brown polymers called *melanoidins* give coffee its rich brown color, dense mouthfeel, and stable espresso crema.
3. **Harmonizes Acidity:** Softens harsh raw chlorogenic acids into smooth, balanced sweetness.

### Roasting Control: Fast vs. Slow Maillard Phase
* **Extended Maillard Time (Slower Heat):** Builds higher body, rounder mouthfeel, and deeper chocolate/nutty/malty base tones (ideal for espresso blends).
* **Short Maillard Time (Faster Heat):** Preserves vibrant floral and fruit acids, highlighting delicate origin terroir (ideal for filter coffees).
