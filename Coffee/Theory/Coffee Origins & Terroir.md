#coffee/theory #note

In coffee, terroir describes how geographic microclimates, volcanic soils, elevation, botanical cultivars, and indigenous post-harvest traditions converge in the cup. Recognizing geographic origins provides an empirical framework to predict acidity structure, sweetness intensity, and tactile mouthfeel prior to roasting or brewing.

---

## 1. Macro-Regional Profiles

The world's coffee-producing lands within the Bean Belt divide into three primary sensory macro-regions:

- **Africa & Arabia:** The evolutionary origin of *Coffea arabica*. Coffees exhibit vibrant, multi-layered acidity (citric, malic, phosphoric), pronounced floral aromatics (jasmine, bergamot), and distinct fruit notes ranging from crisp citrus to jammy red berries.
- **Central & South America:** The global volume center of specialty coffee. Profiles emphasize sweetness, equilibrium, and clarity, featuring milk chocolate, roasted hazelnut, brown sugar, and stone fruit notes with balanced malic or citric acidity.
- **Asia-Pacific:** Dominated by island microclimates and regional wet-hulling methods. Profiles are known for full syrupy body, subdued acidity, and savory, herbaceous, earthy, and cedarwood notes.

---

## 2. Key Origin Breakdown

### Ethiopia
As the genetic birthplace of Arabica, Ethiopia harbors thousands of uncataloged heirloom cultivars (*Ethiopian Heirloom*).
- **Washed Ethiopias (Yirgacheffe, Guji, Sidama):** Characterized by delicate, tea-like bodies, fragrant floral perfumes (jasmine, lavender, orange blossom), and bright citrus fruit (bergamot, lemon peel, peach).
- **Natural Ethiopias (Harrar, Guji, Sidama):** Sun-dried intact cherries develop dense, syrupy mouthfeels with dominant notes of ripe blueberry, strawberry jam, winey stone fruit, and dark chocolate.

### Kenya
Kenyan specialty coffees grow at extreme elevations (1,700–2,200m) in rich phosphoric volcanic soils surrounding Mount Kenya.
- **Cultivars:** SL-28, SL-34 (selected by Scott Agricultural Laboratories for drought tolerance and cup quality), Ruiru 11, and Batian.
- **Processing:** Rigorous double-washed processing (fermentation followed by underwater soaking channels).
- **Sensory Character:** Bold, complex, winey acidity driven by phosphoric acid. Dominant notes include blackcurrant, ruby red grapefruit, blackberry, and savory sweet-tomato undertones.

### Colombia
Grown along three mountain chains (*cordilleras*) of the Andes, Colombian coffees benefit from varied microclimates and dual annual harvesting periods.
- **Cultivars:** Caturra, Castillo, Colombia, Typica.
- **Sensory Character:** Exceptional balance and versatility. Features medium-to-rich body, approachable orange/citrus acidity, and sweet profiles centered on caramel, panela (raw cane sugar), milk chocolate, and red apple.

### Brazil
The world's largest coffee producer, dominated by vast plateau estates in Minas Gerais (Sul de Minas, Cerrado Mineiro) and São Paulo (Mogiana).
- **Cultivars:** Bourbon, Mundo Novo, Catuaí.
- **Processing:** Predominantly natural (dry-processed) or pulped natural (*cereja descascada*).
- **Sensory Character:** Very low acidity, heavy mouthfeel, and comfort-driven flavor profiles of roasted peanut, hazelnut, milk chocolate, and molasses. Because of high solubility and consistent crema production, Brazilian coffees serve as the foundational base for traditional espresso blends.

### Indonesia (Sumatra)
Indonesian island coffees, particularly from the Lake Toba and Aceh regions of Sumatra, are shaped by volcanic soils and the regional **Giling Basah** (wet-hulling) method.
- **Cultivars:** Typica, Ateng, Tim Tim.
- **Sensory Character:** Viscous, heavy, syrupy body with muted acidity. The cup presents deep rustic notes: cedarwood, pipe tobacco, dark cocoa, forest floor, clove, and herbal spices.

### Central America (Guatemala & Costa Rica)
Grown along volcanic mountain ranges with high mineral content.
- **Guatemala (Antigua, Huehuetenango):** Grown in volcanic basins and dry high-altitude valleys. Delivers full, velvety bodies with crisp green apple acidity, bittersweet cocoa, toffee, and subtle smoky or floral undertones.
- **Costa Rica (Tarrazú, West Valley):** Pioneers of micro-mill innovation and honey processing. Showcases bright, crystalline citric acidity, lighter body, and clean profiles of orange blossom, honey, apricot, and green tea.
